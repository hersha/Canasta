Your turn

Draw from the stock, meld whatever you want to meld, then discard one card to finish. Instead of drawing from the stock you can try to take the discard pile — see below for what that takes.

Wild cards

Twos and Jokers are wild. A wild card stands in for any rank — three 9s and a wild is four 9s, and the wild counts as the fourth 9.

There is a limit on how hard you can lean on them. A set you put down must have more natural cards than wilds — two naturals will carry one wild, three naturals will carry two, and never the other way round. The wild canasta is its own thing and sits outside that rule.

They are the most double-edged cards in the deck:

  • They are the dearest cards to be caught holding. A 2 is against you in hand, a Joker .
  • Any canasta with a wild in it is dirty — instead of the a natural one pays. One wild is all it takes to cost you .
  • But seven wilds together is a wild canasta: , the biggest single score on the board, and worth four natural canastas.

Discarding one freezes the pile, so a wild is also the cheapest way to shut the pile down when someone is circling it.

What the cards are worth

These are the card points you count up at the end of a round: everything you have melded on the table counts for you, everything still in your hand counts against you.

Taking the discard pile

Two ways in, and either one gets you the whole pile. Both of them put the top card straight onto the table — you never take the pile and sit on it:

  • Hold two natural cards matching the top card. Those two and the top card go down together as a meld, there and then. You cannot take the pile and keep them in your hand — if you are not melding them, you are not taking the pile.
  • Or have a meld already down that the top card plays onto, and play it on.

Whatever is underneath the top card goes into your hand.

Freezing the pile

Discarding a wild card or a red 3 freezes the pile. While it is frozen there is only one way in — two naturals from your hand. Extending a meld you already have down will not get you a frozen pile.

Red 3s

Red 3s are bonus cards — they are never melded into a rank like everything else, they just get laid down in front of you and count. Each is worth , and at or more the whole lot doubles. Discarding one freezes the pile.

Get one down and it works for you. Be caught still holding it when the round ends and it is worth only — against you, like any other card in your hand.

The catch: if your side finishes the round without a single canasta, your red 3s count against you instead — at the same value, doubling included. Four red 3s and no canasta is .

Black 3s

Black 3s are worth . They are the safe discard: a black 3 on top stops the next player taking the pile. Almost always they show up as points against you, sitting in your hand when the round ends.

There is one exception, and it is the only time a black 3 is ever melded: if you are holding of them, you can play them down as a set to go out. Go out that way and you get no discard — the black 3s are the last of your hand. On the table they count for you, apiece, rather than against you.

Going out

Your side needs canastas down before anyone can go out. Going out ends the round for everybody, and everything still in the other hands counts against them.
